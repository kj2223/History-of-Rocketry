# Expanded campaign target: WAC Corporal to Apollo, 1945–1969

Scope revision, 29 October 2026. **This is the campaign roadmap, not a claim that the complete campaign is implemented.** The standalone 1945–49 flight prototype, 1958 research/recruitment chapter and Apollo prototype remain separate. See [the early-rocketry play guide](PIONEERS_1945_1949.md) for the implemented first chapter. Mercury flights are the pending flight-layer extension; the new scope includes them rather than replacing them.

## Experience and player role

Begin with a small experimental rocket programme under university/Army sponsorship. Recruitment, instrumentation, manufacturing access, test resources and uncertain evidence constrain what the team can attempt. A successful launch can still fail to return useful measurements. Early flights must be playable events, not background text after a research progress bar.

The player follows programme and institutional leadership as responsibility grows. Before 1958, this is not the NASA Administrator's office. NASA's formation changes the remit and organisation; it does not erase the earlier record or automatically transfer every unrelated military/university programme into one agency. The exact institutional lineage and transfers must be explicit in the scenario.

Over time the player appoints leaders, grants funded authority and manages several programmes concurrently. Staff choose routine engineering investigations and execution within that remit. Operational teams retain launch/flight safety authority. The modern, readable UI remains the presentation direction; historical interface skins remain deferred.

## Campaign progression

| Period | Programme focus | Change in responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| 1945–1949 | Tiny Tim/WAC Corporal experimental flights; instrumentation, repeatability, recovery and the route toward Bumper staging experiments | Lead a small research programme; know the people and review early evidence directly |
| 1950–1958 | Sounding-rocket capability, larger launch systems, tracking and satellite projects; selected paths toward Vanguard/Explorer | Build specialist groups, negotiate sponsored scope and manage shared resources |
| 1958–1963 | NASA formation, Mercury hardware, escape/qualification launches, suborbital and orbital human spaceflight | Appoint agency leadership and delegate programmes while retaining funding and policy responsibility |
| 1961–1966 | Gemini development, endurance, rendezvous, docking and EVA; Apollo development proceeds concurrently | Manage interdependent programmes and decisions whose consequences appear years later |
| 1966–1969 | Apollo ground qualification, uncrewed and crewed flights through Apollo 11 | Carry the organisation's accumulated work into high-stakes flight operations and the landing campaign |

These are overlapping workstreams, not a compulsory queue of disconnected eras. Mercury does not end in May 1961, and Gemini does not belong to the 1958–1961 historical period. Reference dates establish context; simulated dates depend on funding, work and readiness.

## First additional playable slice: 1945 WAC flight series

The first early-rocketry build should be small enough to verify and must include actual launches:

1. An inherited experimental project, a small persistent cast, aggregate supporting staff, limited facilities and a finite sponsor-funded mandate.
2. A staged test campaign: booster-only trials, representative inert-load flights, partially fuelled research flights and full sounding-rocket attempts. These reflect the historical development pattern; individual outcomes are not scripted.
3. Broad player decisions about research objectives, payload/instrument priorities, design margins, resources and reporting boundaries. The technical team proposes the detailed work.
4. Delegated preparation and repeat attempts that consume real hardware/work-package commitments. One mission's observations can inform subsequent configuration revisions.
5. A watchable countdown, ascent and observation/recovery sequence, with transcript and measurement provenance. Missing or unreliable instrumentation remains visible uncertainty. Do not present nominal estimates as measured telemetry.
6. A continuation review recording what was demonstrated, what remains disputed, the hardware lineage, team responsibilities, funds and unfinished work. No automatic technology points or reputation rewards.

Bumper and later satellite programmes follow only once that loop works. Do not claim the whole 1945–1969 target is playable merely by adding mission names or moving the start date.

## Continuous-state requirements

- Use a common simulation clock with overlapping research, procurement, production, testing and operations.
- Track evidence and configuration lineage rather than one universal technology level. WAC experience can contribute relevant knowledge; it does not directly qualify a Saturn V or grant a universal reliability bonus.
- Preserve named people's actual work, appointments, changes in responsibility and eventual career transitions. Do not fabricate 1945 histories for the existing 1958 cast. Keep the wider workforce aggregated.
- Model new mandates, institutional transfers and funding sources explicitly. The date alone must not create money, hardware, employees or successful missions. Existing obligations survive institutional transitions unless an explicit disposition changes them.
- Keep observed results, staff interpretations and underlying simulation reality separate across handoffs. The existing public handoff is a review document, not a sufficient replacement for validated private state.
- Preserve independent operational authority and seeded outcomes. Playback speed, observer location and presentation pauses must not alter physical results.
- Maintain distinct save versions and migrations. Keep the current prototypes usable until their systems have a tested integration path; do not reset player accomplishments at each chapter boundary.

## Delivery sequence

1. Specify and build the 1945 flight loop with a deliberately limited vehicle and test library.
2. Extend the evidence/configuration model toward 1946–1949 research and staging, then selected 1950s launch/satellite programmes.
3. Connect the NASA-formation organisation model to real Mercury development and launches. Retain the pending Mercury extension as a required part of this work.
4. Add the missing 1961–1966 Mercury/Gemini bridge and overlap it with Apollo development.
5. Integrate the existing Apollo programme after testing causal continuity, funding conservation, staff histories and migration through every institutional boundary.

Detailed contractor markets, every historical launch, complete military missile programmes, exhaustive personnel, elaborate physics and period UI evolution are not prerequisites for the first flight slice. Each delivery must contain a clear playable loop and an updated feature coverage record.

## Historical anchors

The White Sands museum describes the September–October 1945 sequence of booster-only, inert-load, partial-charge and full WAC tests: [Corporal family history](https://wsmrmuseum.com/2023/03/07/the-corporal-family-of-rockets-and-missiles/amp/). JPL describes WAC's university/Army context and the initial White Sands work: [JPL programme history](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/videos/corporal-americas-first-guided-ballistic-missile/).

Sources use “first launch” for different test configurations. Scenario records should distinguish the September Tiny Tim trials from the October full WAC flights instead of collapsing them into one supposedly exact first-flight event.
