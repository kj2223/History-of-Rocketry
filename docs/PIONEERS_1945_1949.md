# Early rocketry playtest · 1945–1949

This standalone prototype starts on 1 September 1945 and runs through the end of 1949. It is a fictional university/Army-sponsored research project inspired by the WAC Corporal and Bumper development sequence. You are its programme lead, not NASA Administrator. Existing 1958 and Apollo saves are separate and are not modified.

## Launch

Run `python -m per_aspera --pioneers`, double-click `Launch 1945 Prototype.cmd`, or use the dedicated `PerAspera-1945-Portable.exe`. The portable opens the modern browser workspace and saves beside the executable in `PerAsperaData/saves`. Source launches use the repository's `saves` directory. Save & settings supports resuming a listed 1945 save, exporting the public record, and restarting into a separate save while preserving the previous one.

## First flight

1. Open **Your team** to see four inherited colleagues and three optional recruits. Recruitment runs alongside flight preparation. Their retainer covers the chapter.
2. In **Flight programme**, authorise one **Tiny Tim booster trial**. Choose balanced preparation, additional flight margins or stronger measurement coverage. The quote updates before authorisation. These are programme priorities; Ellis selects the detailed test and preparation procedures.
3. Leave range release delegated or retain a direct review. Press **Next report / event** to reach the preparation boundary, then open **Range observation**. Delegated release begins playback automatically; direct review returns a report before release.
4. Watch or adjust presentation speed. Preparation advances at one day per real second at Auto; the initial release/ignition interval runs at one simulated second per real second, then six. Pause is available. Next report / event deliberately skips to the next authoritative event.
5. Read and acknowledge unexpected observations and the final flight report. Vega retains range authority. Observation pauses add no in-world intervention time. Ask Ellis contextual questions; replies use available observations and remain in the archive.
6. A usable record opens the next scoped objective. An inconclusive or failed attempt can be repeated with revised preparation. Funding three rounds reserves a common envelope, but each report returns for acknowledgement before the team begins the next round.

## Progression and decisions

The available sequence is booster trial → inert-load WAC → limited-duration WAC → full WAC → improved WAC → Bumper interface demonstrator → powered Bumper. This is a selected experimental programme, not a recreation of every historical launch. It includes expendable hardware, scoped preparation, optical/instrument observations, recovery and configuration-specific causal records.

Improved WAC opportunities open in June 1946, interface demonstrations in May 1948, and powered Bumper in January 1949. These are simplified scenario windows, not guaranteed historical schedules. **Next research opportunity** advances only when the workshop/range is free. It does not grant capability, staff or funding. You can continue useful repeat flights during the intervening years.

Chen strengthens future vehicle preparation, Brooks strengthens instrument preparation and recovery, and Reed strengthens staging work. Their appointments cannot change an already completed preparation or flight. Recruits are fictional; the scenario does not attribute these decisions to historical people. Aggregate workshop and range personnel are not individually simulated.

Balanced preparation is cheaper. Extra flight margins improve the relevant preparation but retain limited instrumentation. Measurement priority strengthens independent measurement channels and recovery preparation. Neither guarantees a flight result. Hidden engineering state is separate from the measured/estimated record and staff interpretations; your own working interpretation can be recorded independently.

## Money

The initial illustrative no-year research allocation is **$1,200,000**. There is no separate discretionary reserve, annual top-up or recurring payroll. This deliberately small institutional model predates the later NASA budget model.

| Objective | Balanced / round | Extra margins or measurement / round | Balanced preparation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Booster trial | $30,000 | $42,000 | 13 days |
| Inert-load trial | $38,000 | $50,000 | 17 days |
| Limited-duration WAC | $58,000 | $70,000 | 23 days |
| Full WAC | $80,000 | $92,000 | 29 days |
| Improved WAC | $90,000 | $102,000 | 37 days |
| Bumper interface | $157,000 | $169,000 | 60 days |
| Powered Bumper | $197,000 | $209,000 | 70 days |

Extra margins add seven days to balanced preparation; measurement adds five. Recruitment costs $42,000–$48,000 each and takes 30 days. All costs include the scoped aggregate labour and hardware. These are gameplay costs, not historical price estimates.

A balanced single-attempt sequence costs $650,000 before recruitment/repeats; measurement priority throughout costs $734,000. All three recruits cost $135,000. That leaves an initial margin for failures, but a difficult programme can exhaust it.

Authorising a remit reserves its complete envelope. Preparation moves the per-round obligation into a contract; completing it settles that obligation once. Launch and reporting do not charge it again. Release returns only unused rounds; committed preparation continues. Every commitment, settlement, release and award has a ledger entry showing remaining available funds.

After three flight records, a research continuation can be requested from 1946 ($500,000 if approved). From 1948, useful improved-WAC evidence supports a staging proposal ($1,000,000 if approved). Each is a single seeded sponsor decision and can be refused; there is no automatic income or repeat-until-success request. If resources are insufficient, reduce round count, release unused authority, wait for an eligible proposal, or close with incomplete objectives. Readiness evidence cannot be purchased away.

## Presentation and limitations

- The mission engine progresses in integer seconds with deterministic milestones. Bunker/radar observation uses a quarter-second point-mass trajectory with powered flight, coast, staging and impact. The model omits drag, wind, changing mass and Earth curvature; it is not an exact historical reconstruction or archival footage.
- Each flight samples a simplified vehicle, measurement and recovery outcome once at release. Preparation and specialist appointments affect those probabilities. There is no propellant flow, aerodynamic solver, detailed failure tree or independently simulated V-2 supply chain.
- The bunker has a limited sightline. The radar view plots five-second noisy range/elevation samples and retains the last fix when contact is lost. Height is radar-derived; the highest valid sample is not a confirmed apogee. Without radar reception, no altitude is invented. Previously completed records retain their earlier estimates unchanged.
- Event/flight records retain remit and preparation links. Subsequent preparations link earlier attempts on the same objective. Named staff have roles and expressed opinions; expanded relationships, careers and memory modelling remain deferred.
- Range observation uses persistent widgets and appended transcript entries, retaining the question, focus and reading position during ticks. Other document views refresh on new events when no field is focused. No Tkinter or era-interface work is included.
- The shared preparation/range team is one stream. Recruitment can run in parallel. Larger programme capacity and contractor procurement are deferred.
- Saves contain private simulation state and must not be confused with the public export. There is no handoff/merge into 1958, Mercury or Apollo yet. Closure preserves outstanding obligations for review, not a false completed programme.
- Cached 3D launch films are proposed, not implemented. See [the observation model and rendering direction](WHITE_SANDS_OBSERVATION.md).

## Historical anchors

The [White Sands LC-33 report](https://wsmrmuseum.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/LC-33-Report-June-2020.pdf) distinguishes 1945 booster, dummy, partial-charge and full WAC flights, and later research rounds. The [Army's Corporal history](https://history.redstone.army.mil/miss-corporal.html) records the June 1947 Bumper authorisation and May 1948 interface flight. [NASA's historical data book](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/sp-4012v1.pdf) records the February 1949 Bumper-WAC altitude achievement. These anchor the progression; neither dates nor successful outcomes are imposed on the player.
